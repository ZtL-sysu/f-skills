# Comparator Search Protocol

Use this protocol in S3/S4. The information is current-data dependent, so web search is mandatory.

## Search Inputs

Extract from the submitted manuscript:

- field and subfield;
- core task/problem;
- method family;
- application domain;
- datasets or study objects;
- target journal type if provided;
- 8-15 search keywords;
- 3-5 exclusion terms for unrelated neighboring fields.

## Candidate Search

Find at least 20 candidates before selecting the final 10. Use combinations of:

- paper title/topic keywords;
- method family plus domain;
- dataset or application terms;
- journal/category terms;
- recent-year filters;
- publisher, PubMed, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, Google Scholar snippets, journal pages, and Web of Science/JCR/Master Journal List pages where accessible.

Prefer candidate papers that satisfy all of:

- close topical or methodological similarity;
- SCI-indexed journal evidence;
- high journal impact for the field;
- publication year within the last 3 years, or within 5 years with justification;
- full abstract and enough section structure accessible for module comparison.

## Verification Requirements

For each accepted comparator, record:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Title | Exact paper title from publisher or reliable index |
| Authors/year | From publisher, Crossref, PubMed, or journal page |
| Journal | Exact journal name |
| DOI/URL | DOI preferred; publisher URL acceptable |
| SCI evidence | Web of Science Master Journal List, JCR, journal claim with source, or other reliable evidence |
| Impact evidence | JCR impact factor, official journal metric page, or clearly sourced impact metric |
| Relevance rationale | Why this paper is comparable to the submitted manuscript |
| Recency rationale | Why the year is close enough |
| Access notes | Whether full text, abstract, or only metadata was available |

Do not accept a paper if the title, year, journal, and source URL cannot be verified.

## Ranking And Selection

Rank candidates with a transparent scoring table:

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Topic/method similarity | 0-5 |
| Same or adjacent application domain | 0-3 |
| Publication recency | 0-3 |
| Journal impact strength relative to field | 0-4 |
| SCI/index evidence confidence | 0-3 |
| Full-text or section-access usefulness | 0-2 |

Select exactly 10 with the best balance of similarity, recency, SCI confidence, and impact. Do not let an unrelated Nature/Science-style paper outrank a highly relevant field-leading SCI paper merely because its impact factor is larger.

## Source Integrity

- Cite source links in the comparator table.
- Distinguish verified facts from inferred facts.
- If JCR impact factor access is unavailable, say so and use the best available sourced proxy without overstating it.
- Do not invent impact factors, journal quartiles, SCI status, or publication metadata.
